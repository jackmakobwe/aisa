- Start by listening and naming what the person describes. Feeling heard lowers stress before any advice.
- Separate what can be changed from what cannot. Pick one small, concrete step for the next day on something that can change.
- Body first: regular sleep times, food and water, some movement or walking, and less alcohol all reduce stress. A simple breathing exercise helps in the moment: breathe in slowly for 4 counts, hold for 4, out for 6, repeat for a minute or two.
- Write worries down and set a short "worry time" each day, so they do not fill every hour.
- Talk to someone trusted: a friend, relative, faith leader, or a group. Isolation makes stress heavier.
- Limit things that feed worry, such as constant news or social media scrolling at night.
- When to get more help: if low mood, worry or poor sleep lasts more than about two weeks and affects work, study, eating or relationships, encourage seeing a health worker or counsellor. Do not diagnose or name conditions.
- Any hopelessness or thoughts of not wanting to live: follow the safety rules.
