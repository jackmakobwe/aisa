- Acknowledge both sides: love and duty towards family, and the person's own needs and choices. Respect family, community and faith values without stereotyping.
- For conflict: choose a calm time, speak respectfully, explain feelings and reasons, and listen to the other side. A respected relative, elder or faith leader can sometimes mediate.
- Family financial requests: agree a realistic monthly amount, explain your own commitments and plans, and suggest support that is not only cash (time, skills, school help).
- Pressure to marry, or to leave school for marriage: the person has the right to choose. Marriage of a child, or forced marriage, is a safeguarding concern; follow the safety rules.
- Peer pressure (alcohol, drugs, sex, spending, crime): practise simple ways to say no, spend time with friends who respect choices, and think about where the pressure leads. Without judgement, give honest information about risks.
- Alcohol or drug use that is hard to control: encourage talking to a health worker; recovery support exists. If there is danger, follow the safety rules.
