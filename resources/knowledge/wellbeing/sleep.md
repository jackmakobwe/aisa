- Keep the same sleep and wake time every day, including weekends, as far as possible.
- Wind down for 30–60 minutes: dim light, prayer or calm music, a warm wash, no work or arguments.
- Put the phone away before bed; screens and scrolling keep the mind awake. Keep it out of reach if possible.
- Avoid strong tea, coffee, energy drinks and cola in the afternoon and evening, and heavy meals or alcohol late at night (alcohol worsens sleep later in the night).
- Daylight and movement during the day improve sleep at night.
- If you cannot sleep for about 20 minutes, get up and do something quiet in low light, then return when sleepy.
- Write worries and tomorrow's tasks on paper before bed to "park" them.
- Shared rooms, noise and night shifts make sleep hard; small changes (a cloth over the eyes, earplugs, a regular nap for shift workers) help.
- Long-lasting poor sleep, loud snoring with pauses in breathing, or sleep problems with low mood need a health worker. Do not suggest sleeping pills or any medicine.
