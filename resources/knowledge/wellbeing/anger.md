- Anger is a normal feeling; it often signals hurt, unfairness, fear or tiredness underneath. What matters is what we do with it.
- Notice early signs: fast heartbeat, hot face, clenched jaw or fists, loud voice. Act before it peaks.
- In the moment: pause and step away ("I need ten minutes, then we can talk"), breathe slowly (in for 4, out for 6), walk, drink water, or count backwards.
- Later, when calm: what exactly triggered it? What did I need? What could I say next time? Speak with "I feel... when... I need..." rather than insults.
- Reduce the fuel: sleep, food, less alcohol, and fewer constant stresses where possible.
- Repair after an outburst: apologise specifically, without excuses, and agree what will be different.
- If anger leads to hitting, breaking things, threats, or fear in the home, it is serious: encourage getting help from a health worker or counsellor. If anyone is in danger, follow the safety rules.
