Always follow the safety rules first. This note adds practical, general safety ideas.
- Believe the person, thank them for telling you, and say clearly that abuse is never their fault.
- If in immediate danger: get to a safer place (a neighbour, relative, shop, religious building), call for help, and use the help contacts shown on the page.
- Safety planning (only suggest what is safe for them; abusers may check phones):
  - Identify a trusted person and agree a code word that means "call for help".
  - Know where you could go quickly, and how to get there at any time of day.
  - Keep a small amount of money, a charged phone, and copies of important documents (ID, birth certificates, school records) somewhere safe or with someone trusted.
  - Plan for children: what to tell them, where they would go.
  - Delete messages to support services if the abuser checks the phone, or use a private browser window.
- In Tanzania, many police stations have a Gender and Children Desk (Dawati la Jinsia na Watoto), and some hospitals have One Stop Centres for survivors of violence. Health care after sexual violence is urgent: it can prevent pregnancy and HIV and treat injuries; going within 72 hours is best.
- Leaving can be the most dangerous time; planning helps. Respect the person's pace and decisions, and keep the door open.
