- Mental health is part of health, like physical health. Struggling does not mean someone is weak, "crazy" or has failed in faith.
- Signs that professional help would be useful: low mood, worry, or changes in sleep, appetite or energy that last more than about two weeks and affect daily life; hearing or seeing things others do not; sudden big changes in behaviour; or thoughts of self-harm (follow the safety rules).
- Where to start: a health worker at a local health facility can assess and refer to counselling or specialist services. Some hospitals, universities and NGOs offer counselling.
- What counselling is: confidential conversations with a trained person to understand problems and find ways to cope. It is not judgement or punishment.
- Faith and culture: many people find strength in prayer and community. It can go alongside health care; encourage both rather than choosing between them. Do not argue about beliefs such as witchcraft; focus on the person's distress and practical help.
- Do not diagnose, name conditions, or suggest any medicine. If the person is on medicine, encourage talking to the prescriber before changing anything.
