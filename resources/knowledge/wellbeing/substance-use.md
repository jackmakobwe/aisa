- Respond without judgement. People often use alcohol or drugs to cope with stress, pain, boredom, or pressure from friends.
- Signs it has become a problem: needing more to feel the same, trying and failing to cut down, money problems, missing work or school, conflict at home, risky behaviour, or feeling sick without it.
- Practical steps: set a clear goal (stop or cut down), notice triggers (people, places, feelings), plan alternatives, tell someone supportive, avoid keeping it at home, and count money saved.
- Stopping heavy, daily alcohol use suddenly can be dangerous (shaking, confusion, fits). Encourage seeing a health worker before stopping after long heavy use.
- Help exists: health facilities, recovery and sober-living programmes, and support groups. A health worker can explain local options.
- For family members: you cannot control another person's use; set boundaries, protect children and money, and look after yourself.
- Never give dosing or information on using drugs. Overdose, mixing substances, unconsciousness or breathing problems are emergencies: follow the safety rules.
