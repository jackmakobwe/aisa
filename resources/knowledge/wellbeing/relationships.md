Healthy relationships include
- Respect, honesty, trust, and being able to say what you feel without fear.
- Each person keeping their own friends, family, money and decisions.
- Disagreements handled by talking, not by threats, insults or force.

Warning signs of an unhealthy or abusive relationship
- Checking your phone, controlling where you go or who you see, extreme jealousy.
- Taking or controlling your money, stopping you from working or studying (economic abuse).
- Insults, humiliation, threats, or isolating you from family and friends.
- Pushing, hitting, or forcing or pressuring sex. Sex without free consent is abuse, including within marriage.
If these are present, follow the safety rules and do not encourage staying in an unsafe situation.

Boundaries and communication
- Use "I" statements: "I feel hurt when..., I need...". Choose a calm time to talk.
- A boundary is what you will do, not a demand ("If you shout, I will leave the room and talk later").
- Consent means a free, clear "yes" that can be taken back at any time; silence, fear, pressure or being drunk is not consent.

Breakups and infidelity
- Allow the hurt; avoid big decisions in the first days of shock.
- Lean on trusted people, and look after sleep, food and routines.
- Decisions about staying, separating or forgiving belong to the person; offer balanced options and respect their faith and family context.
