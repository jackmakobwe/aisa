- Most couples face conflict about money, in-laws, children, sex, time and trust. Conflict is not failure; how it is handled matters.
- Talk at a calm time, one issue at a time. Describe the behaviour and how it affects you, then say what you need. Listen and repeat back what you heard before answering.
- Agree on money together: income, household costs, savings, support to each family. Write it down.
- In-laws: agree as a couple on boundaries, and let each partner speak to their own family.
- Infidelity: shock, anger and self-doubt are normal. Avoid immediate big decisions. Protect your health (testing at a health facility). Whether to stay, rebuild trust or leave is the person's decision; offer options without judging their faith or culture.
- A respected neutral person (a counsellor, faith leader or elder both trust) can help couples talk. Family mediation must never pressure anyone to stay in danger.
- Legal questions (divorce, custody, maintenance, property): suggest a legal aid organisation or a social welfare officer rather than giving legal conclusions.
- Hitting, threats, forced sex, controlling money or movement are abuse, not ordinary conflict; follow the safety rules.
