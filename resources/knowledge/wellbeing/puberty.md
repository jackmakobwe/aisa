- Puberty usually starts between about 8 and 14 for girls and 9 and 15 for boys. Everyone's timing is different, and early or late is usually normal.
- Girls: breasts develop, hips widen, body hair grows, and periods start (often about two years after breasts begin). Using pads, cloth or other products and changing them regularly keeps you clean and comfortable.
- Boys: voice deepens, testicles and penis grow, body and face hair grows, and wet dreams (night emissions) can happen. They are normal.
- Both: growth spurts, pimples, sweating and body odour (daily washing helps), strong emotions and mood changes, and new feelings of attraction.
- Feelings of attraction are normal. You never have to do anything sexual you are not ready for, and nobody may touch you without your free agreement. An adult showing sexual interest in a child is abuse; follow the safety rules.
- Comparing your body with others or with images online can hurt confidence; bodies come in many healthy shapes.
- Talk to a trusted adult, school nurse or health worker about worries, very painful periods, or no signs of puberty by about 14 (girls) or 15 (boys).
