- Every child develops at their own pace. Ranges are wide, so avoid alarming parents, but encourage a check with a health worker when they are worried.
- Rough guide (general, not a diagnosis): most babies smile by about 2 months, sit without support around 6–9 months, say first words and take first steps around 12 months, and use two-word phrases around 2 years.
- Suggest a clinic or health worker check if a child loses skills they had, does not respond to sounds or their name, is not walking by about 18 months, has no words by about 18 months, or the parent has any strong worry.
- Brain development is fastest in the first years. Talking, singing, reading or telling stories, and playing with everyday objects all help. Responding warmly when the baby cries or babbles builds trust.
- Nutrition: exclusive breastfeeding for about the first six months is widely recommended, then continued breastfeeding with varied foods. Growth monitoring and vaccinations at the clinic are important. Refer feeding or growth concerns to a health worker.
- Do not give doses or medicines. For illness, fever, refusing to feed, or breathing difficulty in a baby, advise going to a health facility promptly.
