- Children benefit when fathers are involved: playing, reading, helping with school, and caring tasks, not only providing money.
- After separation, children do best when parents avoid fighting in front of them, do not speak badly about the other parent, and keep routines predictable.
- Agree arrangements in writing where possible: who pays for what (food, school, health), when the child sees each parent, and how decisions are made.
- Use calm, short, child-focused messages. If talking directly always leads to conflict, a trusted mediator or a social welfare officer can help.
- Child maintenance, paternity and custody are legal matters: suggest a social welfare officer (Afisa Ustawi wa Jamii) at the district or a legal aid organisation, rather than giving legal conclusions.
- Step-families: give children time to adjust, let the biological parent lead discipline at first, and make time for each child.
- If a child is unsafe with a parent, follow the safety rules.
