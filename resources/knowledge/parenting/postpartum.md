- In the first two weeks after birth, many mothers feel tearful, anxious or moody ("baby blues"). Rest, food, help with the baby and kind company usually help it pass.
- Lasting low mood, anxiety, feeling unable to cope, not bonding with the baby, or feeling numb for more than two weeks is common and treatable. It is not a failure as a mother. Encourage talking to a health worker at the clinic.
- Practical support matters: sleep when someone else can watch the baby, accept help with cooking and chores, eat regularly, and talk to someone who listens.
- Partners and family can help by taking night or morning shifts, doing housework, and protecting the mother's rest.
- Thoughts of harming oneself or the baby, confusion, or seeing or hearing things others do not are emergencies: follow the safety rules and encourage urgent health care.
- Fathers can also feel low after a birth; encourage them to talk too.
