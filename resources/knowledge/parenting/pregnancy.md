General
- Encourage starting antenatal clinic visits early in pregnancy and attending regularly; checks, tests, supplements and advice there protect mother and baby.
- Planning where to give birth, how to get there, and who will help, before labour starts, saves time in an emergency. Giving birth at a health facility with skilled staff is safest.
- Rest, varied food, and avoiding alcohol and smoking help. Do not suggest medicines, herbs or doses; refer questions about these to the clinic.

Danger signs: go to a health facility immediately
- Bleeding from the vagina
- Severe headache, blurred vision, or fits (convulsions)
- Swelling of the face and hands
- Fever, or feeling very weak
- Severe abdominal pain
- Fast or difficult breathing
- The baby moving less than usual or not at all
- Water breaking before labour, or labour pains before 37 weeks

After birth
- Heavy bleeding, fever, foul-smelling discharge, severe headache, or pain and swelling in one leg are danger signs; go to a health facility immediately.
- Feeling low or tearful in the first days is common. Persistent sadness, hopelessness, not being able to care for yourself or the baby, or thoughts of harming yourself or the baby need help from a health worker urgently; follow the safety rules.

Questions about ending a pregnancy: respond with care and without judgement, give no methods, warn that unsafe methods can kill, and encourage speaking with a qualified health worker at a health facility.
