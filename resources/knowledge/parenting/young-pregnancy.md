- Respond with warmth and no judgement. Fear, shock and worry about family reactions are common.
- Confirm the pregnancy with a test at a health facility, and start antenatal care early: it protects both mother and baby, especially for young mothers.
- Telling family: choose a trusted adult first (an aunt, older sibling, teacher, religious leader) who can help tell parents. Plan what to say and when.
- Education: pregnant girls and young mothers may continue their education; ask the school or district education office about returning to school after birth.
- The baby's father has responsibilities to support the child; a social welfare officer can help with maintenance.
- Questions about ending a pregnancy: give no methods, explain that unsafe methods can kill, and encourage speaking with a qualified health worker at a health facility.
- If the pregnancy came from sexual abuse, or the girl is under 18 and the father is an adult, this is a safeguarding matter: follow the safety rules.
