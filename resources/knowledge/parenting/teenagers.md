- Teenagers need both connection and limits. Pushing back and wanting independence is part of growing up, not simply disrespect.
- Stay connected: spend regular one-to-one time, show interest in their world (friends, music, phone), and listen without immediately lecturing.
- Set a few clear, fair rules together (curfew, phone use, school work), explain the reasons, and agree consequences in advance. Apply them calmly and consistently.
- Know their friends and where they are, without spying on everything. Trust can grow as they show responsibility.
- Talk openly and early about relationships, consent, sex, pregnancy, alcohol and drugs, and online safety. Young people who get honest information from adults usually make safer choices.
- Praise effort and good choices; avoid comparing them with siblings or other children.
- Warning signs needing more attention: sudden change in friends, dropping grades, withdrawing, unexplained money or gifts, secretive phone use with adults, signs of substance use, or talk of hopelessness. Talk calmly; get help from the school, a health worker, or follow the safety rules if there is risk.
- An adult in a "relationship" with a child or demanding sex is abuse: follow the safety rules.
