- Every child can learn and grow. A disability is not caused by the mother, by sin, or by curses; avoid blame and stigma.
- Early checks help: if development seems different, a health worker can assess and refer for therapy, hearing or vision checks, or specialist services.
- Education: children with disabilities have a right to education. Ask the school or the district education office about inclusive schools, special units and support.
- At home: follow the child's interests, use simple routines, pictures and gestures, celebrate small progress, and give choices.
- Parents need support too: parent groups and disability organisations share practical tips and reduce isolation. Share care with family where possible.
- Children with disabilities are at higher risk of abuse; teach body safety in ways they understand and keep close watch on caregivers.
- Do not suggest medicines, herbs or cures. For fits or other urgent symptoms, go to a health facility.
