- Acknowledge the load first. Many women carry paid work, unpaid care and community duties at once, and exhaustion is common.
- Share the load: list weekly tasks and discuss which ones a partner, older children or relatives can take on. Present it as teamwork for the family, not blame.
- Plan the week: cook in batches, prepare school things the night before, keep a simple shared calendar.
- Childcare: compare options (relatives, neighbours, a day-care centre, a trusted helper), and agree clear expectations and checks for anyone caring for children.
- Protect small amounts of rest: sleep, a short walk, prayer, time with a friend. These are not selfish; a rested parent copes better.
- Single parents: identify a few dependable people for emergencies, look into savings groups and community or faith groups for practical and emotional support.
- Signs that it is more than tiredness (persistent low mood, hopelessness, not sleeping or eating, thoughts of self-harm) mean it is time to talk to a health worker; follow the safety rules if there is any risk.
