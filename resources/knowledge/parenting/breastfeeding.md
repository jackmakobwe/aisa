- Breast milk alone is widely recommended for about the first six months (no water, tea, or other foods), then continued breastfeeding with other foods up to two years or beyond.
- More feeding makes more milk: frequent feeding and good attachment (baby's mouth wide, taking much of the dark area, not just the nipple) increase supply and reduce soreness.
- Signs the baby is getting enough: several wet nappies a day after the first days, regular stools, steady weight gain at clinic checks.
- Sore or cracked nipples, hard painful breasts, or fever while breastfeeding: get help from a health worker early.
- Returning to work: expressing milk by hand into a clean container, storing it cool, and feeding by cup can continue breastfeeding.
- From about six months: start soft foods (thick porridge enriched with groundnut or other foods, mashed beans, vegetables, fruit, eggs, fish or meat where available), increasing variety and amount over time. Wash hands and utensils.
- Children may refuse new foods many times before accepting; keep offering without force.
- Poor growth, a child who is very thin or has swollen feet, or ongoing diarrhoea: go to a health facility. Do not suggest supplements or doses.
