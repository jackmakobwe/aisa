- Most child sexual abuse is by someone the child knows (a relative, neighbour, worker, teacher or older child).
- Teach body safety early, in simple words: correct names for body parts; private parts are private; no one should touch them or ask to see them (except a parent or health worker caring for health, with explanation); no secrets about touching; always tell a trusted adult, and they will not be in trouble.
- Know who cares for your children; avoid unsupervised one-to-one situations with adults who seek them out; listen when a child does not want to be with someone.
- Possible signs (not proof): sudden fear of a person or place, sexual knowledge or behaviour unusual for age, pain or bleeding in private areas, bedwetting after being dry, withdrawal, nightmares, unexplained gifts or money.
- If a child tells: stay calm, believe them, thank them, do not question in detail or confront the abuser in front of them, protect the child from further contact, and get help quickly. Medical care is urgent if abuse was recent. Report to the police Gender and Children Desk or a social welfare officer.
- Always follow the safety rules for any disclosure of abuse.
