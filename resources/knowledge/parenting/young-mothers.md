Speak warmly and without any judgement about age or circumstances. The site has a Mama Care page with this information; you may suggest it.

Why extra care for young mothers
- A body under about 18 is still growing. Risks are higher: anaemia, high blood pressure, long or blocked labour, and early or small babies. Early and regular antenatal care (ideally at least eight contacts) and giving birth at a health facility reduce these risks.

After birth (first six weeks)
- Postnatal check-ups: first day after birth, around day 3, days 7–14, and at 6 weeks.
- Bleeding like a heavy period that slowly lightens over a few weeks is normal. Keep stitches or a caesarean wound clean and dry; change pads often.
- Danger signs for the mother (go immediately): heavy bleeding or large clots, fever or chills, bad-smelling discharge, wound red or leaking pus, severe headache or blurred vision or fits, pain or swelling in one leg, breathing difficulty or chest pain, thoughts of harming herself or the baby.

Obstetric fistula
- Constant leaking of urine or stool after a long or blocked labour. Young mothers are at higher risk.
- It is a medical injury, not her fault and not a curse. It can usually be repaired by surgery; in Tanzania repair is offered at specialist hospitals, often free. Encourage telling a health worker and asking for a referral. Do not name specific hospitals.

Spacing the next pregnancy
- Advise waiting at least two years after a birth before the next pregnancy.
- Pregnancy is possible before periods return, even while breastfeeding. Breastfeeding alone protects only if the baby is under six months, fed only breast milk day and night, and periods have not returned.
- Many family planning methods are safe while breastfeeding; a health worker or youth-friendly service can explain options. The choice is hers; no one may force sex or pregnancy.

School and future
- Young mothers in Tanzania may return to school after giving birth; suggest asking the school or district education office. Vocational training (such as VETA) and short courses are other routes.
- The baby's father has a duty to support the child; a social welfare officer can help.

Safety
- If the pregnancy came from force, or from an adult when she was under 18, it is abuse: follow the safety rules.
