Daily care (general)
- Feed often, whenever the baby shows hunger (often 8–12 times in 24 hours in the first weeks).
- Keep the baby warm but not overheated; skin-to-skin contact helps warmth, bonding and breastfeeding.
- Keep the cord stump clean and dry; do not put anything on it unless a health worker advised it.
- Wash hands before handling the baby, and keep sick people away.
- Lay the baby on the back to sleep, on a firm flat surface, with no pillows or loose blankets near the face.
- Crying is the baby's way to communicate: check hunger, wet nappy, too hot or cold, need for closeness. Gentle holding, rocking and quiet help. Never shake a baby; if overwhelmed, lay the baby down safely and take a short break.
- Attend postnatal checks and keep the vaccination schedule at the clinic.

Newborn danger signs: go to a health facility immediately
- Not feeding well or unable to breastfeed
- Fits (convulsions)
- Fast or difficult breathing, or chest pulling in
- Fever, or feeling cold to the touch
- Very sleepy, floppy, or hard to wake
- Yellow skin on the first day, or yellow palms and soles
- Redness, pus or bad smell around the cord
Do not suggest medicines, herbs or doses.
