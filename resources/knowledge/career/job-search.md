CV
- Keep it to one or two pages: contact details, a short profile, education, experience (including volunteering, internships, family business, leadership in school or church/mosque groups), skills (languages, computer, driving), and referees.
- Use clear headings and simple formatting. Describe what you did and achieved, with numbers where possible.
- Adjust the CV and letter for each job; repeat key words from the advert.

Applications
- Apply to many suitable jobs, keep a list of where you applied, and follow up politely.
- Use networks: tell people you are looking, attend career fairs, and ask for informational chats.
- Internships and volunteering often lead to jobs and build experience.

Interviews
- Research the organisation, prepare examples of times you solved a problem, worked in a team, or learned something quickly.
- Arrive early, dress neatly, bring copies of your documents, and prepare one or two questions to ask.
- After rejection, ask for feedback where possible; rejection is normal and not a measure of worth.

Job scams: warning signs
- Paying a fee to apply, to be "registered", for training or uniforms, or for a guaranteed job.
- Offers that are too good to be true, interviews only on chat apps, or pressure to send documents and money quickly.
- Overseas job offers: verify the recruitment agency is licensed by the relevant government authority, never hand over your passport to an agent, get a written contract you understand, and tell family exactly where you are going. Being promised one job and given another, having documents taken, or not being free to leave are signs of trafficking; follow the safety rules.
