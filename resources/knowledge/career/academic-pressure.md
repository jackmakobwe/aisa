Study strategies
- Short, regular sessions (25–45 minutes with short breaks) work better than long last-minute nights.
- Active study: test yourself with past papers, explain topics aloud or to a friend, make summary notes and flash cards, rather than only re-reading.
- Plan backwards from the exam date: list topics, give each a day, and leave time for revision.
- Sleep, food, water and some movement improve memory and concentration.
- Study groups help when they stay focused; ask teachers about topics that are unclear early.

Pressure and results
- Acknowledge the feelings: fear of disappointing parents, comparison with others, worry about the future.
- One exam result does not decide a whole life. Options can include re-sitting, private candidate exams, supplementary exams at university, vocational training (such as VETA), diplomas and later entry routes.
- Help the person prepare how to talk with parents about results or about a different path: share facts, show a plan, and listen.
- Hopelessness, panic attacks, not sleeping or eating for a long time, or thoughts of self-harm around exams need a trusted adult or health worker; follow the safety rules if there is risk.
