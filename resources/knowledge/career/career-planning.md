A simple process
1. Know yourself: what subjects or tasks you enjoy, what you are good at, what people ask your help with, and what conditions matter (location, income, stability).
2. Explore options: talk to people already doing the work, visit workplaces, read about the training and real day-to-day tasks, not only the job title.
3. Compare routes: university degrees, diplomas, vocational and technical training (for example VETA centres for trades such as electrical, plumbing, tailoring, mechanics, ICT and hospitality), apprenticeships, self-employment, or combinations of these.
4. Check practical facts: entry requirements, cost and length of training, funding and loan options, and whether graduates actually find work.
5. Make a 3–6 month plan with small concrete steps: a short course, volunteering, an internship, building a portfolio, or starting a small side business.

Encouragement
- There is rarely one "right" career. Many people change direction; skills carry across.
- Failing an exam or not getting the first choice is not the end. Re-sitting, alternative courses, vocational routes and private candidate options exist.
- Families often have strong views about careers. Suggest ways to have a respectful conversation: share research, show a plan, and listen to their concerns.
