Steps
1. Start from what the person knows, can do and can reach: skills, location, people they know, the time they have.
2. Check demand before spending: who will buy, how often, at what price, and who already sells it nearby. Talk to about ten possible customers.
3. Start small and test: buy a small quantity, sell it, learn, then reinvest. Avoid spending all the capital on equipment or decoration at the start.
4. Price for profit: price = cost of goods + other costs (transport, packaging, rent, fees) + profit. Beginners often forget their own transport and time.
5. Keep records from day one in a notebook: what was bought, what was sold, money in, money out. Keep business money apart from personal money.
6. Reinvest profit at first, and take a small fixed "salary" rather than spending the takings.
7. As the business grows it may need a local business licence from the council, registration with BRELA, and a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) from the Tanzania Revenue Authority (TRA). Suggest asking at the local council or TRA office.

Ideas for about TSh 100,000 (illustrative only; say so, and adjust to their area):
- Food vending (chapati, maandazi, juice): ingredients, basic utensils, packaging.
- Reselling fast-moving goods bought in bulk (soap, sanitary pads, airtime, sorted second-hand clothes).
- A service using a skill (hair braiding, phone charging, clothing repairs, tutoring).

When giving a plan, show: start-up costs, expected sales per day, cost and price per item, profit per item, and how many sales are needed to recover the capital. Check every calculation.

Be honest: many small businesses earn little at first and no profit is guaranteed. Encourage learning from people already doing similar work, and saving in a group for growth capital rather than taking expensive loans.
