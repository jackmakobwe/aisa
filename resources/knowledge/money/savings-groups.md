- Rotating groups (upatu, mchezo, merry-go-round): members contribute a fixed amount each period and one member receives the total each round. Simple, but no interest is earned and it depends fully on trust.
- VICOBA (village community banks) and similar savings and loan groups: members buy shares regularly, the group lends to members at an agreed interest, and profits are shared, often yearly.
- SACCOS: registered savings and credit cooperatives with formal membership, savings and loans; generally larger and regulated.
- Before joining, check: written rules (constitution), how money is recorded and kept (records, a locked box with several key holders, or a bank account), how loans and interest are decided, what happens if someone does not repay or leaves, and that meetings and records are open to all members.
- Good practice: transparent meetings, receipts or record books for every member, rotating leadership, and never keeping all the money with one person.
- Borrow from the group only for a clear purpose with a repayment plan.
- Problems such as missing money or a leader refusing to share records: raise them in a full meeting; for registered groups, the cooperative officer at the district can advise.
