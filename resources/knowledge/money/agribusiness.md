- Treat the farm as a business: write down every cost (seeds, fertiliser, feed, vaccines, labour, transport, rent) and every sale, so you know the real profit per season or batch.
- Before starting, check the market: who will buy, at what price, and at what time of year. Prices are often lowest right after harvest; storage or staggered planting can help.
- Start small and learn: for example a small batch of chickens, or a small plot, before expanding.
- Get advice from the local agricultural or livestock extension officer (afisa ugani) about suitable varieties, animal health and good practices.
- Animal health: vaccination, clean water and housing prevent many losses. Disease can wipe out a batch, so do not invest everything in one batch.
- Reduce risk: mix crops or activities, save part of each harvest income for the next season's inputs, and avoid expensive loans before you have a proven market.
- Groups and cooperatives can help with bulk buying of inputs and selling at better prices.
- Example calculation format: (number sold × price) − total costs = profit. Check the numbers and say they are an example.
