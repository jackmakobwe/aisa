Before borrowing
- Know the total cost: the total amount to repay, every fee, the interest per month (not only per year), late-payment penalties, and what happens to any security (dhamana).
- Small instant phone loans and informal lenders can be very expensive once fees and short terms are added up. Calculate the total repayment before accepting.
- Borrow for things that can pay the loan back (stock that sells quickly, tools for work) or for real emergencies. Not for daily spending, celebrations or betting. Never borrow to put money into a scheme that promises high returns.
- Do not take a new loan to repay an old one unless it is clearly cheaper and there is a realistic plan to finish both. This is how debt spirals start.
- Signing as a guarantor (mdhamini) can mean paying the whole loan yourself.

Already in debt
- List every debt: who, how much, monthly payment, cost, penalties.
- Keep paying at least the minimum on all of them; put any extra on the most expensive first.
- Talk to the lender early. Many agree a new schedule if asked before payments are missed.

Protection
- Banks, microfinance providers and other lenders in Tanzania are licensed by the Bank of Tanzania. Suggest checking that a lender is licensed, and reading and keeping the written agreement.
- Threats, public shaming, or taking someone's documents to force payment are not acceptable. Suggest advice from a legal aid organisation or the authorities.
- People under 18 should not take loans; involve a trusted adult.
