Warning signs
- High returns, fast, with "no risk" or "guaranteed" profit (for example 20% a week, or doubling money in a month). Real investments never guarantee high returns.
- You earn mainly by recruiting other people, or must pay to join or "upgrade" a level (pyramid or Ponzi scheme). Early members are paid with later members' money until it collapses.
- Pressure to act now, secrecy, or "only for selected people".
- Payment to a personal mobile money number or in crypto, not to a registered company.
- It is unclear what the business actually does or sells; proof is screenshots of "profits" and luxury lifestyles.
- Messages like "I sent money to your number by mistake, please send it back", fake prizes, fake jobs, or anyone asking for a PIN or confirmation code. Never share a PIN or code with anyone, including people who say they are from a mobile network or bank.

How to check
- Investment firms and fund managers should be licensed by the Capital Markets and Securities Authority (CMSA). Banks, microfinance and lenders by the Bank of Tanzania. Check on the regulator's official website or at its office, not through a link the promoter sends.
- Search the company name with words like "scam" or "utapeli", and ask someone knowledgeable you trust.
- Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and never borrow to invest.

If someone has been scammed
- Stop sending money, even if asked for a "release fee" to get earlier money back. That is a common second scam.
- Report the transaction to the mobile money provider or bank straight away, and report to the police with screenshots and transaction details.
- Be kind. Scams are designed to fool careful people, and shame stops people from reporting.
