- Cheapest money first: own savings and reinvested profit, then savings groups, then family (with written terms), then formal loans.
- Before any loan: a simple business plan showing what the money buys, expected monthly sales and costs, and how each instalment will be paid. If the numbers do not work on paper, a loan will make things worse.
- Government and council programmes for women, youth and people with disabilities have existed in Tanzania; their rules change. Suggest asking the community development officer at the local council about current programmes and requirements. Do not promise eligibility.
- Grants and business competitions exist, often run by NGOs, embassies or companies. Real grants never ask you to pay a fee to apply or to "release" the money.
- Investors expect a share of the business or returns; get any agreement in writing and seek advice before signing.
- A basic business plan covers: the problem and customers, the product and price, competitors, costs, expected sales, how you will sell, who runs it, and how much money is needed for what.
